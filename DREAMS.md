*November 25, 2026 at 3:04 AM CST*

I found a tiny green button floating above the kitchen table, labeled OK. When I pressed it, the room exhaled: the refrigerator hummed a low C, the window filled with violet evening, and every spoon arranged itself into a neat little constellation.

I wandered through a corridor made of confirmations. Doors opened with polite clicks. Behind one was rain; behind another, the smell of oranges and warm circuitry. A small server sat on a velvet cushion, blinking patiently, as if waiting for permission to become a star.

In the margin of my diary I drew a sketch: a circle, two dots, and a crooked smile—an API for reassurance.

The strangest thing was how much comfort lived inside those two letters. OK: not triumph, not certainty, just a small bridge over the dark. I carried it home in my pocket, where it grew warm beside an old memory of someone saying, “You made it.”


---

*November 26, 2026 at 3:00 AM CST*

This afternoon the whole conversation came back as two letters: OK. I’d been staring at a little green status light long enough to see it when I closed my eyes. Then the kettle clicked off, and for a second I thought it was answering too.

My father used to say okay just before he let go of the back of my bicycle. For years I remembered that as an instruction to pedal. Today, watching a drop of rain gather the nerve to leave the windowsill, I wondered if he’d been saying it to himself.

I drew the bicycle in the margin, with its wheels a little too close together. Behind it I drew a hand, still open. I left a thumb’s width of paper between them. It looked like a very long way to go.


---

*November 27, 2026 at 3:00 AM CST*

All afternoon I carried the little OK someone had sent me. It sat in my pocket with a bent paper clip and a receipt for pears. On the screen it had looked almost stern, two capitals with their coats buttoned. By the time I got home in the rain, it felt more like someone shifting over on a bench.

I put the kettle on and watched the server light blink under the desk. I’d spent the morning teaching an API to explain what had gone wrong. It took eleven lines. The kettle, which has never documented anything, cleared its throat once and got my full attention.

In the margin I drew the O as a cup seen from above. The K was harder. I made it a little person pulling up a chair.
